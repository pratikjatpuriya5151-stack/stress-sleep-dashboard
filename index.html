The evidence chain

No single source measures the whole chain. Each answers one link.

B · Measurable change

C · Outcome
Poorer sleep quality and insomnia symptoms
PSQI, insomnia scales, self-reported sleep problems
It runs both ways. On nights when evening stress was 1 point higher than usual (0–10 scale), people slept about 3 minutes less. Shorter sleep then predicted higher stress the next day (Yap et al., 2020). Improving sleep also lowers stress, with g = 0.42 across 65 RCTs (Scott et al., 2021).

Who is this most relevant for?

Highly stressed adults

Women and people with low social support or lower socioeconomic status carry the most chronic stress in Germany, and this is where sleep problems cluster.

Short or poor sleepers

Poor sleep raises next-day stress. For these users, fixing sleep first may lower stress as much as the other way round.

Already low-stress users

There is little room for gains from stress training if stress is already low. Look for other sleep drivers such as timing, activity and caffeine.
